About the System & Project Architecture

Academic Internship Project: Artificial Intelligence & Meteorological Data Integrity.

1. Problem Statement & Objectives

Meteorological data is the cornerstone of civil aviation, agriculture, disaster management, and renewable energy forecasting. However, automated weather stations (AWS) deployed in the field suffer from physical sensor degradation, communication dropouts, ice accretion, electrical spikes, and potential cyber telemetry spoofing.

This project builds an autonomous, multi-layered AI verification pipeline that audits time-series weather datasets, flags physical impossibilities, isolates spatial/temporal outliers, and calculates defensible integrity confidence scores without human intervention.

2. The 5-Tier Verification Framework
Tier 1: Physical Boundary & Meteorological Plausibility

Enforces physical boundaries (e.g., rainfall >= 0, 0% <= humidity <= 100%, -50°C <= temp <= 60°C). Any breach is an immediate hardware failure or packet corruption.

Tier 2: Persistence & Sensor Freeze Detection

Detects Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC) lockups where a sensor repeats identical decimal values across 12+ consecutive hours.

Tier 3: Machine Learning Spatial Outlier Suite

Runs Isolation Forest (path-length tree isolation), Local Outlier Factor (k-NN density deviations), and Rolling Z-Score (|Z| > 3.0σ).

Tier 4: Deep Learning Temporal & Structural Modeling

LSTM Recurrent Neural Networks learn the diurnal cycle to predict the next hour's temperature, flagging high residual errors. Deep Autoencoders compress multi-variate correlations and flag impossible joint distributions.

Tier 5: Threat & Incident Severity Scoring

Consolidates results into a weighted 0-100 integrity score and outputs structured alerts (LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH, CRITICAL).

3. Meteorological Anomaly vs. Malicious Tampering

A core academic contribution of this work is the strict distinction between natural severe weather and artificial data manipulation: A severe storm naturally creates high wind gusts accompanied by heavy rainfall and humidity jumps (verified as a Meteorological Outlier). Conversely, an artificial injection or faulty sensor might report a +25°C temperature spike with zero change in surrounding parameters or negative precipitation (classified as Potential Tampering or Hardware Corruption).
